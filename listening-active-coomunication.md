## Question 1
**What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening? (Minimum 6 points)**
1. Avoid getting distracted by your own thoughts. Focus on the speaker and topic instead.
2. Try not to interrupt the other person. Let them finish and then respond.
3. Use door openers. These are phrases that show your are interested and keep the other person talking.
4. Show that you are listening with body language.
5. If appropriate take notes during important conversations.
6. Paraphrase what other have said to make sure you are on the same page.
## Question 2
**According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening? (Write in your own words, use simple English)**

1. Listen carefully: Pay full attention to what the speaker is saying.
2. Understand the message: Focus on the speaker's thoughts and feelings.
3. Rephrase: Repeat the speaker's message in your own words.
4. Show empathy: Understand and acknowledge the speaker's emotions.
5. Verify understanding: Ask questions to confirm that you understood correctly.
6. Avoid judging: Don't interrupt, criticize or give advice immediately.
Main goal: Make the speaker feel understood and avoid misunderstandings.

   ## Question 5
**When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day to day life?**
You act passive when you are afraid of getting into an argument. You stay silent, say "it’s fine" when it is not, and put other people's choices ahead of your own just to keep everyone happy.
## Question 6
**When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day to day life?**
You act aggressive when you lose your temper. This usually happens when you are very stressed, tired, or when you have stayed quiet for too long and finally explode by shouting or demanding your way,.
## Question 7
**When do you switch to Passive-Aggressive communication?**
You act passive-aggressive when you are mad at someone, but you don't want to tell them directly. Instead of talking it out, you give them the silent treatment, make sarcastic jokes, or complain about them to someone else.
## Question 8
**How can you make your communication assertive?**
1. Say "I feel..." — Talk about your own feeling instead of blaming the other person (e.g., "I feel tired" instead of "You are making me mad").

2. Know what you need — Figure out what you actually want (like a break or help with a chore) before you start talking.
+-
3. Start small — Practice asking for small things without saying "sorry," like asking a friend to lower the TV volume.

4. Stay calm — Speak in a normal, calm voice and stand or sit comfortably so you don't seem scared or angry.

5. Don't wait — Tell people what is bothering you right when it happens instead of letting it build up inside.
